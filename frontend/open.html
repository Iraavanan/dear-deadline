The deadline calculator, for your site

Legal aid offices, libraries and community groups may place the small calculator below on their own pages. It asks for the kind of letter and the date on it, and shows the dates. It collects nothing.

Held-out test: how well does software read these letters?

150 more invented letters, written after the reader was finished, with traps that cost people money: a monthly amount that is not the total, a letter with no date, a total that does not match its own figures, and letters that are not overpayment notices at all. Any tool can take the same test, including AI models and letter-explainer apps. A number given where the letter states none is counted as invented.

"They asked again": does a tool mistake a receipt for acceptance?

24 invented Florida SNAP cases where a new notice asks again for proof: two household members, two paydays, a receipt that leaves an item out, a request repeated word for word, and a due date that has passed. The right answers were written before the matcher was built. Three measures: missing items found, wrong-person or wrong-period matches, and receipts treated as the agency accepting a document. The simple checklist rule (anything of the same kind sent counts as done) is scored on the same cases.

How readable are Social Security's overpayment notice paragraphs?

Social Security's own notice guide asks for the sixth to eighth-grade reading level and says to check each paragraph's Flesch-Kincaid grade. We scored the paragraph templates its notice system uses for overpayments, recovery, refunds, waivers and appeals, as published in its public manual.

Using and citing it

The code is under the MIT license. The rule tables and test letters may be reused with credit. If you use them in research, cite: Shirshak J. Thapa, Dear Deadline: rule tables and invented test letters for benefit notices, 2026.

The rules are a reading of Social Security's public pages by an independent project. They are not official and not legal advice. Check the source link on each row before relying on it.
